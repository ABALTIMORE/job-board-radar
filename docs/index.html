Job Board Radar Set it up

Stop checking 20 careers pages every morning.

Make a list of the companies you'd love to work for. Job Board Radar checks their careers pages every day and gives you a short list of new jobs that fit you, often before they show up on the big job sites.

For job seekers who know where they want to work. Free, and no coding needed.

Set it up (about 10 minutes) See an example

How it works

You tell it two things once. After that, it does the checking for you.

1

List your companies

The places you'd actually want to work, like Stripe, Airbnb, or Figma.

2

Say what you're looking for

Job titles, where you want to work, and the lowest salary you'd take.

3

Get new matches every morning

Only jobs you haven't seen yet, each with a link to apply. You decide where to apply.

What you get each morning

A short list like this one. Yesterday's jobs don't come back, so it stays short.

Set it up in your web browser

About 10 minutes, nothing to install. You'll need a free GitHub account. GitHub is the free website where this tool lives, and it will run your daily check for you.

  1. Make your own copy

    Sign in to GitHub, open the Job Board Radar page, and click Fork near the top right, then Create fork. You now have your own copy to change.

  2. Add your companies

    In your copy, click the file companies.example.csv, then the pencil icon (Edit). Replace the list with your companies, one per line: the name, then the website. Keep the first line as it is.

    name,website,priority Stripe,https://stripe.com,1 Figma,https://www.figma.com,2

    The number at the end is optional. Use 1 for your top choices so they show up first. Click Commit changes to save.

  3. Say what you're looking for

    Open config.example.json the same way and change three things:

    • Job titles: the words in quotes after "title_include". For example, change "\\bproduct manager\\b" to "designer". You can type plain words; the \\b marks are optional.
    • Where: "location" can be "US" (the US plus US remote) or "any".
    • Lowest pay: "pay_floor" is the lowest yearly salary you'd consider, like 120000. Jobs that don't list pay are kept, so you don't miss them.

    Keep the quotes and commas where they are, then click Commit changes.

  4. Turn on the daily check

    Click the Actions tab and then I understand my workflows, go ahead and enable them. To try it right now, click daily radar on the left, then Run workflow.

  5. Read your list

    In the Actions tab, click the newest run. Your new jobs appear on that page with links to apply. It runs on its own every morning (around 7 to 8am Eastern), so check back each day.

Good to know: your copy is public, so anyone could see your company list and job titles. Don't put anything private in it, like your current employer's name.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

No. It's free, and so is the GitHub account. There are no subscriptions or AI fees.

Does it apply to jobs for me?

No. It finds new jobs and gives you the links. You decide where to apply, and you apply yourself.

Will it work for my companies?

It works for most tech companies and many others: any company whose careers page runs on Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby, three popular hiring systems. A quick check: open a job on the company's careers page. If the link mentions greenhouse, lever, or ashby, it will work. Companies using other systems (like Workday) aren't covered yet, so check those by hand.

Is this allowed?

Yes. It reads the same public job listings that each company shows on its own careers page. It doesn't log in anywhere or copy anything from LinkedIn or Indeed. Please don't run it more than a few times a day.

I'm comfortable with the command line. Can I run it on my own computer?
  1. Install Python 3.9 or newer. Nothing else is needed.
  2. Download the code:
    git clone https://github.com/ABALTIMORE/job-board-radar.git
cd job-board-radar
  3. Edit companies.example.csv and config.example.json as in steps 2 and 3 above.
  4. Run it, then open data/new_jobs.md:
    python job_board_radar.py

More options are in the README.
